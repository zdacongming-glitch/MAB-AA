# Literature and journal checks

Checked on 15 October 2026. The novelty assessment uses a targeted search of directly comparable primary work, not an exhaustive proof of absence.

The [ALL-epsilon paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/edf0320adc8658b25ca26be5351b6c4a-Paper.pdf) was read for its model, Theorem 2.1, discussion of simultaneous-recovery logarithms, and ST2/FAREAST algorithms. Relative thresholds, all-good-arm identification, and the effect of unknown best-value evidence are established prior ideas. The new question here concerns repeatedly required reports and accumulated unresolved arm-time under permanent arrivals.

[Ballooning bandits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10055) already use the permanent expanding-arm model and current-best regret comparator. This manuscript must not claim either as a new formulation. Its arrival-distribution conditions are different from a fixed deterministic schedule with a uniform service requirement.

[Cho et al. (2025)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/cho25a.html) already combine reward-oriented sampling with anytime-valid classification for good-arm identification at a fixed threshold. [Degenne et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/degenne19a.html) already study cost and identification time jointly. [Yang, Tan and Jin (2026)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/24-1612/24-1612.pdf), especially Theorems 3, 5 and 7, treat regret-optimal fixed-confidence best-arm identification and its sampling-time cost. The present manuscript therefore attributes its increment to continual relative-set service, not to the general combination of reward and inference.

[Cheshire et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/cheshire20a.html) study terminal thresholding loss, which differs from cumulative allocation regret and unresolved area. [Malloy and Nowak (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1801) provide sequential simultaneous-recovery precedents with coordinatewise restrictions. Their logarithmic scales are not claimed as new here. The supplied note's partial-recovery lemma is proved directly for adaptive interleaving.

The current [JASA reproducibility guide](https://jasa-acs.github.io/repro-guide/) covers Theory and Methods as well as Applications and Case Studies. It says invited revisions require code, data and workflow; reject-and-resubmit authors may be asked for these materials. A local reproducible package is supplied. The [publisher instructions](https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=uasa20) could not be fully verified through the browser. No current abstract word limit, T&M page limit, or anonymity rule is asserted from an old template. The supplied PDF is an author-review draft, not a claim of portal-format compliance.

The 2026 [anytime GAI paper](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v27/24-0680.html) concerns identifying one arm above a given threshold. It was checked as a nearby task; it does not supply the continual relative-all-arm guarantees used here.
