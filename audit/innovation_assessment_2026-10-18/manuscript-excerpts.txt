56: \begin{abstract}
57: We study reliable maintenance of all relatively near-optimal arms while
58: collecting rewards when new arms arrive exogenously and remain available.
59: Reward laws are stationary, but arrivals change the membership boundary.
60: Reporting progress is measured by cumulative unresolved arm time outside
61: a prescribed boundary band, with simultaneous correctness of all labels.
62: One uncertain arrival can obstruct many earlier certifications. A
63: common-reference lower bound quantifies this effect, and matching
64: worst-schedule area bounds yield an arrival-growth boundary for joint
65: sublinear performance. On specified repeated-update families, temporary
66: membership tasks impose rapid-maintenance regret costs, while separate
67: constructions attain deferred and intermediate service levels. We develop
68: Flexible Certificate-Cost Planning (F-CCP) as a tunable implementation. It
69: combines sequential evidence and local certificate planning with a chosen
70: reward kernel and separate observable budgets for reward and
71: certification. Its guarantees include label reliability, unconditional
72: expected-regret protection, and service-rate area bounds that retain
73: budget rejections. Fixed-checkpoint and activation-budgeted constructions
74: remain distinct analytical witnesses. Frozen paired experiments show
75: lower mean costs in many finite-budget comparisons, together with
76: exceptions, heavier regret tails, and nonzero reporting failures. These
77: results do not characterize a general regret--maintenance frontier.
78: \end{abstract}
79: \noindent\textit{Keywords:} sequential experimentation; arriving arms;
80: near-optimal set maintenance; dynamic regret; confidence sequences.

82: \section{Introduction}
83: \label{sec:introduction}
84: 
85: Sequential experiments often acquire new candidates while earlier ones remain
86: available. An allocation rule must learn which candidates yield high rewards.
87: An experimenter may also need to know which of all available candidates are
88: within a specified tolerance of the current best. These objectives need not
89: favor the same observations. A candidate can be sufficiently inferior to
90: receive little reward-driven exploration and still be difficult to classify
91: relative to the near-optimality boundary.
92: 
93: We study these objectives together under permanent exogenous arrivals and
94: stationary reward laws. If $r^*(t)$ is the largest mean among the arms
95: available at time $t$, an arm is relatively near-optimal when its mean is at
96: least $r^*(t)-\varepsilon$. Reports certify membership, certify exclusion, or
97: leave the arm unresolved. We require one simultaneous correctness event for
98: all labels at all times. To rule out indefinite abstention, we charge the
99: cumulative unresolved time of arms whose means lie more than $\eta$ from
100: the membership boundary. The question is how much regret is necessary, and
101: how much is attainable, under this reporting requirement.
102: 
103: New arrivals affect the task in two ways. An uncertain new candidate may be
104: better than the incumbent by enough to invalidate many existing positive
105: reports. Evidence about that one candidate is then relevant to many
106: reports at once. A later improvement can also make an earlier difficult
107: membership question unnecessary: a candidate close to the old boundary may
108: be far below the new one. Its existing upper confidence bound can be
109: combined with a new reference's lower bound to certify exclusion. Neither
110: effect requires the reward law of an old arm to change.
111: 
112: Our observation model and regret comparator have established precedents.
113: Permanent arrivals form a monotone special case of sleeping-bandit
114: availability \citep{kleinberg2010}; ballooning bandits already compare reward
115: with the best arm currently available \citep{ghalme2021}. Exogenous arrivals
116: differ from drawing arms on demand from a reservoir
117: \citep{10.1214/aos/1069362389} and from the disappearing actions of mortal
118: bandits \citep{NIPS2008_788d9869}. Benchmark improvements here arise from new
119: arms, whereas nonstationary bandits allow the reward distributions of
120: existing arms to change \citep{Besbes2014,Auer2019ADSWITCH}. Sequential
121: allocation and adaptive experimentation have a long statistical history
122: \citep{Robbins1952,LAI19854,Lai1987}; the expanding set of actions retains
123: that allocation problem and adds an evolving inferential target.
124: 
125: For a fixed arm set, identifying every relatively near-optimal arm is the
126: ALL-$\varepsilon$ problem \citep{mason2020}. Joint reward and identification
127: objectives also have established precedents
128: \citep{degenne2019,yang2026,cho2025}. Our task requires continuing reports
129: under arrivals, with all-time label reliability and an explicit cumulative
130: cost for unresolved arm time. The distinction concerns the reporting
131: protocol and its cost, rather than a new arriving-arm model. The simultaneous
132: recovery scales used in our lower bounds build on existing information
133: inequalities and multiple-recovery arguments
134: \citep{kaufmann2016,malloy2014,jamieson2018}.
135: 
136: We give three results about maintenance cost. First, a common-reference
137: lower bound shows how one uncertain arrival can obstruct many other
138: certifications. Its information queue accumulates cost over time within
139: one reward environment. Second, we obtain matching worst-schedule orders
140: for cumulative unresolved area, including finite-horizon truncation, and
141: derive the polynomial arrival boundary for jointly sublinear area and
142: expected regret. This worst-case lower bound already occurs in an initial
143: static batch; the common-reference result describes a separate consequence
144: of continuing arrivals. Third, on a specified repeated-update family,
145: rapid resolution of temporary membership tasks has matching inverse-square
146: regret costs. Allowing a constant-factor larger area budget admits a
147: logarithmic regret upper bound. A nonvanishing-probability lower bound
148: holds in one fixed environment under the same cumulative-area requirement.
149: The deferred endpoint has only an upper bound. A separate activation-budgeted
150: witness covers an intermediate interval on a more strongly separated family;
151: the general causal frontier under overlapping arrivals remains open.
152: 
153: 
154: Fixed checkpoints make attainable costs explicit, but their quotas can
155: consume a moderate budget before useful reports appear. F-CCP uses every
156: observation to update valid reporting evidence and plans one certificate
157: observation at a time. A predictable priority controls the proposed
158: service rate. Separate budgets limit the virtual-index cost of reward
159: choices and of certification, so exploration by the reward kernel does
160: not exhaust the certification account. The resulting guarantees concern
161: reliability, regret protection, and conditional service bounds; the
162: local planning proxy is not a solution of the full statistical
163: allocation problem. The fixed-checkpoint witnesses are retained for
164: their original guarantees in the supplement.

198: \subsection{Observation model and regret}
199: 
200: Let $\mathcal A$ be a possibly infinite set of arms. Arm $i$ arrives at
201: time $\tau_i$, after which it remains available. At every physical time
202: $t=1,2,\ldots$,
203: \[
204:  \mathcal A_t=\{i:\tau_i\le t\},\qquad
205:  1\le K_t=|\mathcal A_t|<\infty.
206: \]
207: Arbitrary finite batches are allowed, including $K_T>T$ at an evaluation
208: endpoint. Each arm has an unknown stationary reward law on $[0,1]$ with
209: mean $r_i$. Its successive potential observations $(Y_{i,n})_{n\ge1}$ are
210: independent and identically distributed, and the streams of different arms
211: are independent. Arrivals are exogenous and independent of both these
212: streams and policy randomization. Unless stated otherwise, each result
213: is for a fixed deterministic arrival schedule.
214: 
215: At time $t$, the policy first receives all current arrivals, then selects
216: one arm $I_t\in\mathcal A_t$, observes one reward $X_{t,I_t}$, and finally
217: issues its report. It uses the observed history
218: \[
219:  H_{t-1}=(\mathcal A_s,I_s,X_{s,I_s})_{s=1}^{t-1}
220: \]
221: and independent randomization. Means, future arrivals, and true membership
222: labels are unavailable to the policy. With
223: $N_i(t)=\sum_{s\le t}\ind\{I_s=i\}$, the physical observation is
224: $X_{t,I_t}=Y_{I_t,N_{I_t}(t-1)+1}$. Write
225: \[
226:  \hat r_i(n)=\frac1n\sum_{s=1}^nY_{i,s},\qquad
227:  r^*(t)=\max_{i\in\mathcal A_t}r_i,\qquad
228:  \Delta_i(t)=r^*(t)-r_i \quad(i\in\mathcal A_t).
229: \]
230: At a deterministic endpoint $T$, cumulative dynamic regret is
231: \[
232:  R_T(\pi)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_{I_t}(t).
233: \]
234: This is a sum of mean gaps, random through the policy's arm selections;
235: $\E R_T$ is its expectation. The quantity does not compare realized
236: reward sequences. Physical time always counts actual observations.
237: 
238: \subsection{Reliable reports and cumulative unresolved time}
239: \label{sec:reporting_task}
240: 
241: For $0<\varepsilon<1$, the target set is
242: \[
243:  \mathcal G_t^\varepsilon=\{i\in\mathcal A_t:\Delta_i(t)\le\varepsilon\}.
244: \]
245: After the current observation, a report partitions all of $\mathcal A_t$
246: into $\mathcal P_t,\mathcal N_t,\mathcal V_t$: certified members, certified
247: nonmembers, and unresolved arms. Define
248: \[
249:  E_{\rm lab}=\{\text{for all }t:\ \mathcal P_t\subseteq\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon
250:                   \subseteq\mathcal A_t\setminus\mathcal N_t\}.
251: \]
252: A policy is $\delta$-reliable if $\Prob(E_{\rm lab})\ge1-\delta$ in every
253: allowed reward environment. A positive report may be withdrawn after a new
254: arrival. On the correctness event, a negative report remains valid because
255: $r^*(t)$ is nondecreasing and the arm's mean is stationary.
256: 
257: Reliability permits all arms to remain unresolved. For a boundary width
258: $0<\eta<1$, define
259: \begin{equation}
260:  \begin{split}
261:  d_i^\varepsilon(t)&=|\Delta_i(t)-\varepsilon|,\\
262:  U_t(\eta)&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A_t}
263:    \ind\{i\in\mathcal V_t,d_i^\varepsilon(t)>\eta\},\qquad
264:  \mathcal U_T(\eta)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}U_t(\eta).
265:  \end{split}
266:  \label{eq:area}
267: \end{equation}
268: The count includes newly arrived arms with no observations and old arms
269: that become unresolved again. Every such arm--time pair is charged; the
270: loss is not limited to the time before a first decision. Within the
271: boundary band, unresolved time is exempt, but any label issued there must
272: still be correct. The true distance $d_i^\varepsilon(t)$ is used only by
273: the evaluator and in the analysis.
274: 
275: Task parameters are fixed within a comparison. Allocation preferences
276: change the strategy, rather than the reporting target. The parameters
277: used below have the following roles.
278: \begin{center}
279: \small
280: \begin{tabular}{lp{.67\textwidth}}
281: \toprule
282: Parameter & Role\\
283: \midrule
284: $\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1)$ & Membership tolerance and exempt boundary width\\
285: $\delta\in(0,1/2)$ & Simultaneous reporting error budget\\
286: $\lambda_t\in[0,1]$ & Predictable preference for certification\\
287: $p_0\in(0,1),\ k_0>0,\ \nu>0$ & Service floor, backlog scale, and planning price floor\\
288: $\mathsf B,\ \theta>0$ & Reward-score kernel and its temperature\\

291: \bottomrule
292: \end{tabular}
293: \end{center}
294: The fixed-checkpoint witnesses may prescribe other precisions before
295: observations. Preference switching does not change
296: $\varepsilon,\eta,\delta$, create observations, or reset accumulated
297: evidence or budget accounts.
298: 
299: 
300: The main requirement at a fixed $T$ is
301: \begin{equation}
302:  \E_\vartheta R_T\le r,\qquad
303:  \Prob_\vartheta\{E_{\rm lab},\mathcal U_T(\eta)\le a\}\ge1-\delta.
304:  \label{eq:joint_protocol}
305: \end{equation}
306: It combines an expected regret bound with a joint high-probability
307: label--area guarantee. In particular, $\mathcal U_T=o(T)$ requires the
308: time-average number of unresolved separated arms to vanish, rather than
309: only a small unresolved fraction of all arms. The area guarantee is for
310: each deterministic endpoint; it need not hold for every endpoint on one
311: common area event.
312: 
313: For a performance family $\Theta$, let $\mathfrak R_\Theta(a)$ be the
314: infimum of $\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\E_\vartheta R_T$ over policies reliable on
315: the full bounded model and satisfying the probability condition in
316: \eqref{eq:joint_protocol} for every $\vartheta\in\Theta$. The value is
317: $+\infty$ if the feasible class is empty. Full-model label reliability
318: and a family-specific area guarantee are distinct requirements. The
319: lower bounds remain valid when reliability is required only on the
320: lower-bound subfamily.
321: 
322: \subsection{How arrivals change the reporting problem}
323: 
324: For a fixed old arm, $\Delta_i(t)$ is nondecreasing, so its true membership
325: can change only from member to nonmember. Its boundary distance
326: $|\Delta_i(t)-\varepsilon|$ need not be monotone. A new candidate can first
327: create uncertainty about the current best mean, obstructing positive
328: certification of many old arms even when those arms remain truly
329: near-optimal. A sufficiently good new reference can later move the
330: boundary past earlier candidates, making their fine classification
331: unnecessary. Sections~\ref{sec:maintenance} and~\ref{sec:tradeoff} quantify
332: these two effects under the same reporting protocol.
333: 
334: 
335: Arms with close means need not be close to the relative boundary
336: $r^*(t)-\varepsilon$. Conversely, a candidate near that boundary can
337: require fine evidence even when its reward gap is appreciable. The
338: algorithm's observable work set is therefore defined from confidence
339: intervals, not from the evaluator's true gaps or unresolved area.

341: The reporting task need not receive a fixed fraction of every sampling
342: budget. Evidence obtained while collecting rewards can already certify
343: some arms, and a small amount of additional work can complete other
344: reports. Flexible Certificate-Cost Planning (F-CCP) uses one set of lifetime
345: statistics for both purposes. Its service preference controls how often
346: certification is attempted; two separate budgets protect the reward
347: allocation and reserve capacity for certification. The resulting guarantees
348: concern reliable labels, regret order, and service completion. They do not
349: identify the optimal allocation for every joint regret--area constraint.
350: 
351: \subsection{Evidence and current reporting work}\label{sec:fccp-evidence}
352: Order arms by arrival, with a fixed order within each batch. Write
353: $n_i=N_i(t-1)$ and $\Sigma_i$ for the actual count and reward sum available
354: before the action at time $t$. Initially $n_i=\Sigma_i=0$ and
355: $[\ell_i,u_i]=[0,1]$. For $\alpha_i=\delta/\{2i(i+1)\}$, invert the
356: beta-mixture process
357: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-mixture}
358:  \mathsf M_{i,n}(m)=
359:  \frac{\operatorname B(\Sigma_{i,n}+1/2,n-\Sigma_{i,n}+1/2)}
360:  {\operatorname B(1/2,1/2)m^{\Sigma_{i,n}}(1-m)^{n-\Sigma_{i,n}}},
361:  \qquad m\in[0,1],
362: \end{equation}
363: where $\operatorname B$ is the beta function, distinguished from the
364: optimal mean $r^*(t)$. The interval is the intersection of
365: $\{m:\mathsf M_{i,n}(m)\le1/\alpha_i\}$ over all observed positive
366: prefixes. At the actual count
367: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-quota}
368:  \rho=\eta/4,\qquad
369:  q_i=\left\lceil\frac{2}{\rho^2}
370:              \log\frac{4i(i+1)}{\delta}\right\rceil,
371: \end{equation}
372: intersect it also with
373: $[\hat r_i(q_i)-\rho/2,\hat r_i(q_i)+\rho/2]\cap[0,1]$.
374: Continue the mixture updates after this checkpoint. The construction is
375: valid for bounded rewards, including nonbinary rewards; its supermartingale
376: argument is given in Supplement~\ref{app:fccp-evidence}. Sequential betting
377: and mixture confidence sequences are established tools
378: \citep{waudbysmith2024betting}.
379: 
380: For a nonempty confidence rectangle define
381: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-gap}
382:  g_i^-=\max\{0,\max_{j\ne i}\ell_j-u_i\},\qquad
383:  g_i^+=\max\{0,\max_{j\ne i}u_j-\ell_i\}.
384: \end{equation}
385: Both bounds are zero when there is only one arm. They are the exact
386: minimum and maximum of the gap over this rectangle, including its
387: self-exclusion constraint. After observing a reward, report
388: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-report}
389:  \mathcal P_t=\{i:g_i^+\le\varepsilon\},\quad
390:  \mathcal N_t=\{i:g_i^->\varepsilon\},\quad
391:  \mathcal V_t=\mathcal A_t\setminus(\mathcal P_t\cup\mathcal N_t).
392: \end{equation}
393: A negative report does not remove an arm from future reward allocation.
394: Among unresolved arms let
395: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-work}
396:  Z_t=\{i\in\mathcal V_t:[g_i^-,g_i^+]
397:                  \subseteq[\varepsilon-\eta,\varepsilon+\eta]\},
398:  \qquad W_t=\mathcal V_t\setminus Z_t.
399: \end{equation}
400: These formulas are also evaluated before the action, after the current
401: arrivals, when choosing that round's service. An arm in $Z_t$ remains
402: reported as unresolved; the rectangle already shows that it incurs no
403: $\eta$-unresolved cost. Recompute the sets after every arrival and
404: observation. An empty interval sets a persistent failure flag: all
405: subsequent reports are unresolved and designated service stops. Reward
406: allocation, its guard, and the actual statistics continue.

408: \subsection{A local certificate plan}\label{sec:fccp-plan}
409: The plan assigns observations a positive price and asks which current
410: pairwise certificate is cheapest to pursue. Its centers and radii are
411: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-planning}
412:  m_i=\Pi_{[\ell_i,u_i]}\frac{\Sigma_i+1/2}{n_i+1},\quad
413:  v_i^{\mathrm{plan}}=\max(m_i-\ell_i,u_i-m_i),\quad
414:  \bar n_i=\max(1,n_i).
415: \end{equation}
416: Here $\bar n_i$ is a planning convention; no fictitious observation enters
417: the confidence interval. For each $i\in W_t$, take as its reference $j$
418: the arm of largest $m_j$ if $\max_km_k-m_i>\varepsilon$; otherwise take
419: the largest-upper-bound arm other than $i$. Resolve reference ties by arrival
420: order. Set
421: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-price}
422:  h_i=.95\max\{|m_j-m_i-\varepsilon|,
423:                    \eta-|m_j-m_i-\varepsilon|\},\quad
424:  c_k=\nu+(1-\lambda_t)\zeta_t(k),\quad
425:  a_k=v_k^{\mathrm{plan}}\sqrt{\bar n_k},
426: \end{equation}
427: where $\nu>0$ and the observable index gap $\zeta_t$ is defined below.
428: Solve the two-coordinate proxy problem
429: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-pair}
430:  \min_{x_i\ge\bar n_i,\,x_j\ge\bar n_j}
431:   c_i(x_i-\bar n_i)+c_j(x_j-\bar n_j)
432:  \quad\text{subject to}\quad
433:   a_i/\sqrt{x_i}+a_j/\sqrt{x_j}\le h_i.
434: \end{equation}
435: The interior solution is explicit; boundary and zero-radius cases appear
436: in Supplement~\ref{app:fccp-evidence}. Among pairs with an available
437: endpoint, use the pair of smallest proxy cost. Within that pair, select
438: the endpoint maximizing $v_k^{\mathrm{plan}}/(\bar n_kc_k)$ among those
439: with $n_k<q_k$ and $x_k>n_k$ (using the actual count). Execute only one pull,
440: then replan. If no pair provides an endpoint, select the widest interval
441: among arms with $n_k<q_k$, with arrival order breaking ties. Call this
442: subroutine $\operatorname{Plan}_t$, and its proposed arm $j_t$.
443: 
444: The optimization in \eqref{eq:fccp-pair} is exact for its continuous
445: proxy. It holds the centers fixed and approximates radius reduction by
446: an inverse square root. Other competitors can remain after one pair is
447: resolved. Thus its solution is a scheduling rule, not an information
448: complexity or a proof of optimal statistical allocation.

450: \subsection{Virtual indices and two budgets}\label{sec:fccp-budgets}
451: The reward guard uses a virtual, clipped MC-MOSS index. Its arrival clock
452: and spectral comparison come from the companion study
453: \citep{temporalgeometry2026}. Initialize $\mathrm{base}=0$. After arrivals,
454: if $K_t>1$ and either $\mathrm{base}=0$ or $K_t-1\ge2\,\mathrm{base}$,
455: set $\mathrm{base}=K_t-1$, $\sigma=t$, and
456: $d_{\mathrm{clock}}=2\,\mathrm{base}$. Retain all observations. With
457: $s=t-\sigma+1$, define
458: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-index}
459:  J_i(t)=\begin{cases}
460:  1,&n_i=0,\\
461:  \min\{1,\hat r_i(n_i)+
462:        \sqrt{[\log\{s/(d_{\mathrm{clock}}n_i)\}]_+/n_i}\},&n_i>0,
463:  \end{cases}\qquad
464:  \zeta_t(i)=\max_jJ_j(t)-J_i(t).
465: \end{equation}
466: When $K_t=1$, set $J_i=1$ and $\zeta_t(i)=0$ without using an
467: uninitialized clock. The virtual index never forces an observation by
468: itself. In particular, $\zeta_t$ is an observable index difference,
469: not realized regret or a difference between two independent runs.
470: 
471: Choose the first initial arm as a counting anchor. Among all other
472: arrived arms, let $k_{\ell,t}$ count current ages $t-\tau_i+1$ in group
473: $0$ (age one), or group $\ell\ge1$ (ages in
474: $(2^{\ell-1},2^\ell]$). The current spectrum and its running maximum are
475: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-spectrum}
476:  \mathcal S_t=\sum_{\ell\ge0}
477:        \min\{2^\ell,\sqrt{k_{\ell,t}2^\ell}\},\qquad
478:  \overline{\mathcal S}_t=\max_{u\le t}\mathcal S_u.
479: \end{equation}
480: The anchor's identity supplies no information about its mean or
481: optimality. Computing \eqref{eq:fccp-spectrum} uses only arrived arms'
482: current ages, with no endpoint or future-arrival input.
483: 
484: Fix $\beta_R,\beta_C\in[0,\infty]$ before the run. Maintain separate
485: ledgers, initially zero,
486: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-ledgers}
487:  C_R(t)=\sum_{u\le t:\,\mathrm{reward}}\zeta_u(I_u),\qquad
488:  C_C(t)=\sum_{u\le t:\,\mathrm{service}}\zeta_u(I_u).
489: \end{equation}
490: Accept a proposed service only if
491: $C_C(t-1)+\zeta_t(j_t)\le\beta_C\overline{\mathcal S}_t$.
492: Otherwise use a reward round and record a denial. On any reward round
493: maximize the selected reward kernel's scores over
494: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-feasible}
495:  \mathcal F_R(t)=\{i:C_R(t-1)+\zeta_t(i)
496:                                   \le\beta_R\overline{\mathcal S}_t\}.
497: \end{equation}
498: A largest-$J_i$ arm has zero index gap, so this set is nonempty. An infinite
499: budget disables the corresponding check, including when the spectrum is
500: zero; it is implemented as \texttt{None}, rather than as budget zero.
501: No ledger is reset at a clock change or preference change.
502: 
503: For example, the default reward scores are one draw per arm,
504: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-score}
505:  \Xi_i(t)\sim\operatorname{Beta}
506:        (1+\Sigma_i/\theta,\,1+(n_i-\Sigma_i)/\theta).
507: \end{equation}
508: For Bernoulli rewards, $\theta=1$ gives standard Beta$(1,1)$ Thompson
509: sampling \citep{thompson1933}; $\theta=.5$ is a colder action rule.
510: For general bounded rewards these fractional-count scores are a
511: sampling heuristic. The evidence always uses the original counts and
512: reward sums. On a reward round draw the full score vector once, then
513: maximize that same vector over \eqref{eq:fccp-feasible}; do not redraw
514: following rejection. UCB1, Growing-MOSS, MC-MOSS, and KL-UCB can also
515: supply reward scores.

517: \subsection{Service preference and executable rule}\label{sec:fccp-modes}
518: Let $\lambda_t\in[0,1]$ be a predictable service preference. Given a
519: proposed service $j_t$, write $b_t=|W_t|$ and
520: $\widehat c_t=\max_i m_i-m_{j_t}$. The adaptive controller uses
521: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-rate}
522:  w_t=p_0+(1-p_0)\frac{b_t}{b_t+k_0}
523:                          \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon+\widehat c_t},
524:  \qquad
525:  p_t=\begin{cases}
526:  0,&b_t=0\text{ or service is disabled},\\
527:  \displaystyle\frac{\lambda_tw_t}{1-\lambda_t+\lambda_tw_t},&\text{otherwise}.
528:  \end{cases}
529: \end{equation}
530: Fixed-rate control instead uses $p_t=\lambda_t\ind\{b_t>0\}$.
531: Service is disabled when the failure flag is set or $\lambda_t=0$.
532: The presets use $p_0=.02$, $k_0=4$, and $\nu=.1$.
533: Few or costly proposed certificates tend to reduce the service rate;
534: a large, inexpensive backlog tends to increase it. The controller sees
535: $W_t$, not the true unresolved area. Neither $\lambda_t$ nor $w_t$ is
536: an estimated optimal multiplier or a forecast of future arrivals.
537: 
538: Use a phase $\phi_0=0$: add $p_t$ each round, and if the result is at
539: least one, attempt one service and subtract one. A denial does not
540: refund this phase. In exact arithmetic the number of attempts through
541: $T$ is $\lfloor\sum_{t\le T}p_t\rfloor$. For fixed $\lambda>0$,
542: on busy rounds,
543: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-pmin}
544:  p_t\ge p_{\min}(\lambda):=
545:           \frac{\lambda p_0}{1-\lambda+\lambda p_0}>0.
546: \end{equation}
547: There is no positive service lower bound at $\lambda=0$.
548: Table~\ref{tab:fccp-modes} separates the four presets. Changing only
549: \texttt{set\_priority(1)} does not disable an existing certification
550: budget. Preference changes occur between completed physical rounds and
551: retain the phase, evidence, counts, and both ledgers.

595:    \EndIf
596:   \EndIf
597:  \EndIf

611:   \State $C_R\gets C_R+\zeta_t(I_t)$.
612:  \EndIf

636: \subsection{Reliability, reward protection, and service completion}
637: \begin{proposition}[Reliable reports]\label{prop:fccp-report}
638: Under the bounded stationary model, every legal predictable choice of
639: preferences, prices, and service actions in F-CCP satisfies
640: $\Prob(E_{\mathrm{lab}})\ge1-\delta$. On a common event
641: $\mathcal E$ of this probability, all confidence intervals cover their
642: means at all observed counts, all reports are correct, and every arm in
643: $Z_t$ lies in the boundary band.
644: \end{proposition}
645: The evidence argument does not require the proxy plan to predict its
646: actual sampling cost. The mixture and checkpoint errors each receive
647: $\delta/2$, and a union bound gives $\mathcal E$.
648: 
649: \begin{proposition}[Compatibility endpoint]\label{prop:fccp-compat}
650: Fix a legal reward kernel $\mathsf B$. If service is off from the start
651: ($\lambda_t\equiv0$), $\beta_R=\infty$, and the complete kernel state,
652: initialization, and random-number source agree with those of
653: $\mathsf B$, the two procedures have identical actions, lifetime counts,
654: and regret on every potential reward realization and arrival schedule.
655: \end{proposition}
656: This is a same-backend pathwise statement. A common seed in different
657: random-number backends does not provide the required coupling. The
658: colder, guarded F-R preset is distinct from this endpoint. Switching off
659: service after using it does not recreate a baseline's independent past.
660: 
661: The regret comparison uses an arbitrary-action inequality,
662: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-optimism}
663:  \E R_T\le\min\left\{T,300\mathcal S_T+
664:                           \E\sum_{t\le T}\zeta_t(I_t)\right\}.
665: \end{equation}
666: Supplement~\ref{app:optimism-gap} proves the bridge from the companion
667: MC-MOSS potential-prefix and arrival-clock bounds to arbitrary actions;
668: the potential-prefix bounds apply to the actual sequence of selected actions.
669: 
670: \begin{theorem}[Two-budget reward protection]\label{thm:fccp-budget}
671: Fix finite nonnegative $\beta_R,\beta_C$ and use legal pre-observation
672: actions. For every deterministic schedule and endpoint,
673: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-regret-finite}
674:  \E R_T\le\min\{T,(300+2\beta_R+2\beta_C)\mathcal S_T\}.
675: \end{equation}
676: If service is off throughout the run, omit $2\beta_C$ even when its guard is disabled.
677: If $\beta_R<\infty$ and $\beta_C=\infty$, then, with
678: $Q_T^{\mathrm{cap}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A_T}q_i$,
679: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-regret-unlimited}
680:  \E R_T\le\min\{T,(300+2\beta_R)\mathcal S_T+Q_T^{\mathrm{cap}}\}.
681: \end{equation}
682: These bounds are unconditional, including reporting failures.
683: \end{theorem}
684: The ledgers satisfy their respective running-spectrum budgets, and
685: $\overline{\mathcal S}_T\le2\mathcal S_T$.
686: Substitution in \eqref{eq:fccp-optimism} proves the finite-budget bound.
687: Unlimited certification costs at most $Q_T^{\mathrm{cap}}$ actual
688: service pulls. Thus F-R has the conservative bound $332\mathcal S_T$,
689: Balanced has $334\mathcal S_T$, and Classification retains the extra
690: $Q_T^{\mathrm{cap}}$ term. The companion spectral minimax lower bound
691: makes the finite-budget regret order sharp, for fixed coefficients;
692: the spectral characterization itself is an existing input
693: \citep{temporalgeometry2026}. None of these bounds compares F-CCP's
694: realized regret with an independently run Thompson sampler.
695: 
696: \begin{theorem}[Area under a positive service rate]\label{thm:fccp-area}
697: Suppose every pre-action busy round has $p_t\ge p_{\min}>0$.
698: Let $D_T^{\mathrm{srv}}$ be the number of service attempts rejected by
699: the certification budget. On $\mathcal E$, simultaneously at every
700: endpoint,
701: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fccp-area}
702:  \mathcal U_T(\eta)\le K_T\min\left\{T,
703:                   \frac{Q_T^{\mathrm{cap}}+D_T^{\mathrm{srv}}+1}{p_{\min}}
704:                                       \right\}.
705: \end{equation}
706: When $D_T^{\mathrm{srv}}=0$, this is
707: $O(\min\{K_TT,K_T^2\eta^{-2}\log(K_T/\delta)/p_{\min}\})$.
708: \end{theorem}
709: Here busy means that the current $W_t$ is nonempty after arrivals and
710: before observation. If every current arm has reached its checkpoint,
711: the gap intervals are narrow enough that $W_t$ is empty. Each successful
712: service therefore advances a finite remaining checkpoint, and phase
713: accounting bounds the number of busy rounds. This proves the theorem
714: without requiring a cost-optimal plan. Classification has $p_{\min}=1$
715: and no refusals, hence the worst-area order matches the general lower
716: bound in its common parameter range. Finite-budget Balanced must retain
717: the denial term; it has no unconditional prescribed-area guarantee.
718: An arbitrary predictable $\lambda_t$ preserves label reliability, but
719: completion speed needs the displayed positive lower bound.
720: 
721: \begin{corollary}[Finite-arm completion]\label{cor:fccp-finite}
722: Suppose $\beta_C>0$ is fixed, busy rounds have a uniform positive service
723: rate, and arrivals eventually stop with finitely many arms. On
724: $\mathcal E$, every arm outside the boundary band eventually receives
725: a definite label. This conclusion holds regardless of continuing
726: reward-kernel exploration and its separate ledger consumption.
727: \end{corollary}
728: A conservative finite-prefix bound, the compatibility proof, and a
729: one-round obstruction to exact all-environment dominance appear in
730: Supplement~\ref{app:fccp-guarantees}. Completion on a fixed-confidence
731: event concerns the boundary band; it is not unconditional almost-sure
732: consistency for the exact oracle set.

736: \subsection{One uncertain arrival can obstruct many reports}
737: 
738: The first lower bound retains one reward environment throughout the time axis.
739: It therefore measures a cumulative burden without adding the worst cases of
740: incompatible experiments. Fix
741: $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, $0<\delta\le1/16$, and $0<\eta<\varepsilon/2$.
742: An initial reference has Bernoulli mean $1/4+\varepsilon/2$. Candidates arrive
743: on any prescribed schedule. In environment zero each has mean $p=1/4$;
744: environment $j$ changes only candidate $j$ to mean $p+2\varepsilon$.
745: All candidates are good in environment zero, with boundary distance
746: $\varepsilon/2$. After candidate $j$ arrives in environment $j$, every other
747: candidate is bad.
748: 
749: Let $M_t$ count arrived candidates and $a_t=M_t-M_{t-1}$. Define
750: \begin{align}
751:  q_-&=\left\lfloor\frac{\kl(1/4,\delta)}
752:                               {\kl(1/4,1/4+2\varepsilon)}\right\rfloor,
753:  &Q_t&=(Q_{t-1}+q_-a_t-1)_+,\quad Q_0=0,\notag\\
754:  \mathcal B_T&=\sum_{t\le T}(M_t-1)_+\ind\{Q_t>0\}.
755:  \label{eq:broadcast_queue}
756: \end{align}
757: This analytical queue is an information-capacity certificate: arrivals add
758: $q_-$ units per candidate, while only one observation is possible per round.
759: It is distinct from F-CCP's phased service rule and from the
760: fixed-checkpoint witness's FIFO queue. It imposes no pathwise sampling
761: quota on every candidate. In the stated
762: range $q_-\ge2$.

764: \begin{theorem}[Broadcast uncertainty]
765: \label{thm:broadcast}
766: For every policy reliable on environment zero and all its one-candidate
767: alternatives,
768: \[
769:  \E_0\mathcal U_T(\eta)\ge(3/4-\delta)\mathcal B_T.
770: \]
771: If $\mathcal B_T>0$, then
772: \[
773:  \Prob_0\{\mathcal U_T(\eta)\ge\mathcal B_T/4\}\ge1/4.
774: \]
775: These conclusions still hold when the policy knows the full arrival schedule,
776: the horizon, and the reference distribution.
777: \end{theorem}
778: 
779: At a busy time, some deterministic candidate label has expected sample count
780: below $q_-$. Changing that candidate alone has too little history KL to permit
781: positive certification of another candidate with probability at least $1/4$.
782: Reliability also bounds incorrect negative reports. The remaining probability
783: must be unresolved. The same reference environment supports this argument at
784: every time; the difficult label is selected from expected counts rather than
785: from a favorable realized history. The supplementary proof includes the
786: bounded-variable step needed for the probability statement.
787: 
788: If $m$ candidates have arrived by time $s$, the queue obeys the deterministic
789: inequality
790: \begin{equation}
791:  \sum_{t\le s+qm-1}(M_t-1)_+\ind\{Q_t(q)>0\}
792:                   \ge\frac{q-1}{2}m(m-1),\qquad q\ge2.
793:  \label{eq:queue_area}
794: \end{equation}
795: The factor $m^2$ reflects the number of affected reports as well as the work
796: needed to exclude high-quality alternatives. The subtraction of one unit in
797: $q-1$ accounts for the last observation of each busy period: reports are issued
798: after service.

857: \subsection{Growth rates and the limits of total-count criteria}

859: \begin{corollary}[Arrival-growth boundary]
860: \label{cor:growth}
861: Fix the task parameters of Theorem~\ref{thm:broadcast} and one infinite arrival
862: schedule. If a policy is reliable for every reward environment and has a
863: deterministic envelope $a_T=o(T)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:joint_protocol} in
864: every environment, then $K_t=o(\sqrt t)$. Conversely,
865: $K_T^2\log(eK_T)=o(T)$ suffices for one horizon-free policy to have both
866: $\E R_T=o(T)$ and such a sublinear area envelope. In particular, for
867: $K_t\asymp t^\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1$, simultaneous attainment is possible if
868: and only if $\alpha<1/2$.
869: \end{corollary}

871: The necessity follows from \eqref{eq:queue_area}. For sufficiency, the general
872: checkpoint completion bound in Supplementary Lemma~\ref{app:completion_general}
873: applies throughout $0<\eta<\varepsilon/2$, together with
874: Supplementary Proposition~\ref{prop:reporting}. This argument does not require the narrower
875: range $\eta\le\varepsilon/32$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:worst_area}. The exponent
876: statement leaves a logarithmic gap for general critical schedules.
877: 
878: Within the parameter range of Theorem~\ref{thm:worst_area},
879: \eqref{eq:worst_area} also gives a uniform terminal-count statement: over all
880: schedules with $K_T\le k(T)$, sublinear expected regret and sublinear service
881: envelopes are jointly attainable if and only if
882: $k(T)^2\log k(T)=o(T)$. Here the worst schedule can change with $T$.
883: This statement concerns a different schedule class from the single infinite
884: schedule in Corollary~\ref{cor:growth}.

887: schedule. For example, batches of size

890:  \qquad s_n=2^{2^n},

902: \subsection{A repeated-update family}
903: 
904: We keep the reward environment, arrival schedule, endpoint, and reporting
905: parameters fixed when comparing two area budgets. Choose integers $J\ge1$,
906: $M\ge\max\{2,J\}$ and
907: \begin{equation}
908:  0<\varepsilon\le1/8,\quad 0<\delta\le1/16,\quad
909:  0<v,\quad Jv\le\varepsilon/4,\quad 0<d\le v/32,\quad\eta=d/2.
910:  \label{eq:family_parameters}
911: \end{equation}
912: At $a_e=1+eH$, $e=0,\ldots,J$, a reference with mean $b_e=1/2+ev$
913: arrives. For each $e<J$, the same batch contains $M$ candidates with means
914: \begin{equation}
915:  r_{e,k}=b_e-\varepsilon+\sigma_{e,k}d,
916:            \qquad \sigma_{e,k}\in\{-1,+1\}.
917:  \label{eq:family_means}
918: \end{equation}
919: All rewards are Bernoulli and all arms remain available. The last batch
920: contains only its reference, and $T=(J+1)H$. The family $\Theta$ includes
921: every sign configuration and every within-batch permutation. The policy is
922: not told which arm is the reference. Lower bounds may disclose those
923: identities and the schedule.
924: 
925: In its birth window a candidate is good exactly when its sign is positive.
926: Its reward gap is $\varepsilon-\sigma_{e,k}d$, while its distance to the
927: membership boundary is $d$. At the next update its gap becomes
928: $\varepsilon+v-\sigma_{e,k}d$, so both signs are bad, with boundary distance
929: at least $v-d$. Every reference remains good: the final good set consists of
930: all references. The tasks that distinguish the candidate signs expire even
931: though their arms remain available. The restriction $Jv\le\varepsilon/4$
932: limits the number of updates at any fixed positive improvement size.

934: \subsection{Low area forces enough temporary tasks to finish}
935: 
936: The lower-bound ingredient permits arbitrary interleaving and stopping. For
937: $m$ independent uniform signs with observation laws $P_i^+,P_i^-$, put
938: \[
939:  \kappa=\max_i\max\{D(P_i^+\Vert P_i^-),D(P_i^-\Vert P_i^+)\}
940:                                                     \in(0,\infty).
941: \]
942: If a bounded stopping time has at least half the signs determined with prior
943: probability at least $1/2$, and all configurations have familywise report
944: error at most $\delta\le1/16$, then
945: \begin{equation}
946:  \overline\E\sum_{i=1}^mN_i\ge
947:                  \frac{m}{1024\kappa}\log\frac m\delta.
948:  \label{eq:partial_recovery}
949: \end{equation}
950: Other observations must not encode these signs. The proof factors the

1224: \section{Discussion}
1225: \label{sec:discussion}
1226: 
1227: Reliable maintenance has costs that reward allocation alone does not
1228: measure. An uncertain arrival can obstruct many positive reports, while
1229: a later improvement can end a fine membership task. The general lower
1230: bounds quantify these effects. The worst-area lower bound answers a
1231: different question and is already attained by an initial static batch;
1232: the fixed-schedule and terminal-count growth consequences retain
1233: different quantifiers.
1234: 
1235: F-CCP adds an implementable choice of emphasis to this task. Its evidence
1236: layer uses every observation, its certificate planner proposes a single
1237: next observation, and separate accounts control reward and certification
1238: costs. Finite guards protect the spectral regret order but can reject
1239: service; the area bound retains that rejection cost. Classification
1240: priority with no certification guard achieves the worst-area order,
1241: without being fastest on every instance. A compatibility endpoint
1242: recovers a specified baseline from the start under identical state and
1243: randomization. The default cold-temperature reward mode has a different
1244: contract.
1245: 
1246: The frozen experiments support lower mean reward costs for F-R in most
1247: of the specified comparisons and useful finite-budget trade-offs for
1248: the service modes. They also show an exception in the two-arm close-mean
1249: case, heavier empirical regret tails than Thompson sampling in three
1250: environments, and a nonzero label-error record for the balanced mode.
1251: Pure-reward improvements cannot be attributed to certificate scheduling
1252: that was switched off. The corrected matched-cost ALL comparison is
1253: distinct from the retained superseded controller result. These findings
1254: concern the frozen suite, not uniform dominance or a general frontier.
1255: 
1256: The activation-budgeted witness characterizes an intermediate interval
1257: on a sufficiently separated family, with an additive reward term and
1258: its own information requirements. General causal frontiers under
1259: unknown and overlapping arrivals remain open. The schedule-information
1260: separation does not make every causal comparison impossible. Likewise,
1261: the one-step zero-remainder counterexample leaves room for comparisons
1262: with an explicit adaptation cost or a restricted performance family.
1263: 
1264: Automatic precision, reward kernels with better tail behavior, a faithful
1265: adaptation of the original ALL-$\varepsilon$ algorithms, and large-pool
1266: planning costs need further study. The supplement's eventual exact-set
1267: consistency theorem belongs to a separate revisable multiresolution
1268: procedure under finite final arrivals and strict boundary separation.
1269: F-CCP's coverage-event completion outside a boundary band is a different
1270: statement.

1431: \subsection{The bounded-reward mixture}
1432: Fix a stream of mean $m\in(0,1)$ and let its first $n$ rewards be
1433: $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\in[0,1]$. For $\vartheta\in(0,1)$ put
1434: \[
1435:  f_{m,\vartheta}(x)=
1436:  (\vartheta/m)^x\{(1-\vartheta)/(1-m)\}^{1-x}.
1437: \]
1438: This is the exponential of an affine function of $x$, hence convex.
1439: The chord inequality on $[0,1]$, followed by conditional expectation,
1440: gives
1441: \[
1442:  \E\{f_{m,\vartheta}(Y_n)\mid Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1}\}
1443:  \le(1-m)\frac{1-\vartheta}{1-m}
1444:        +m\frac{\vartheta}{m}=1.
1445: \]
1446: Consequently $\prod_{r\le n}f_{m,\vartheta}(Y_r)$ is a nonnegative
1447: supermartingale. Integrating against the Beta$(1/2,1/2)$ density and
1448: using conditional Tonelli gives another nonnegative supermartingale,
1449: initially one, whose value is
1450: \[
1451:  \mathsf M_n(m)=
1452:  \frac{\operatorname B(\Sigma_n+1/2,n-\Sigma_n+1/2)}
1453:  {\operatorname B(1/2,1/2)m^{\Sigma_n}(1-m)^{n-\Sigma_n}}.
1454: \]
1455: For a nonnegative supermartingale initially one, stop at the first
1456: crossing of $1/\alpha$ before a deterministic integer $L$. The stopped
1457: expectation is at most one, so the crossing probability is at most
1458: $\alpha$. Letting $L$ increase proves Ville's inequality
1459: \[
1460:  \Prob\{\exists n\ge1:\mathsf M_n(m)>1/\alpha\}\le\alpha.
1461: \]
1462: The possible distinction between strict and non-strict crossings is
1463: resolved by taking limits. At $m=0$ or $1$, boundedness forces the stream
1464: to be identically that endpoint almost surely; the formula and inversion
1465: use their limits. In particular, the true endpoint cannot be lost.
1466: 
1467: For a fixed positive count, the log of $\mathsf M_n(m)$ is a constant
1468: minus $\Sigma_n\log m+(n-\Sigma_n)\log(1-m)$. It is convex in $m$,
1469: so its sublevel set is an interval, possibly empty. Intersecting these
1470: sets over observed prefixes retains simultaneous coverage. The union
1471: bound over arrival ranks gives total mixture failure probability at
1472: most $\sum_i\alpha_i=\delta/2$. Adaptive actions reveal prefixes of
1473: these streams; they cannot invalidate an event defined simultaneously
1474: for every potential prefix. Independent policy randomization can be
1475: included in the physical filtration without altering this argument.
1476: 
1477: At the deterministic per-arm prefix $q_i$, Hoeffding's inequality gives
1478: \[
1479:  \Prob\{|\hat r_i(q_i)-r_i|>\rho/2\}
1480:  \le2e^{-q_i\rho^2/2}\le\frac{\delta}{2i(i+1)}.
1481: \]
1482: This prefix is defined even when the actual experiment never reaches it.

3352: observation update the same lifetime statistics, and an active fine
3353: block receives an observation only if it was active before that
3354: observation. Reports use the intersection of available coarse and fine
3355: intervals and the comparisons \eqref{eq:reports}; negative reporting
3356: does not remove reward eligibility. An empty intersection triggers
3357: all-unresolved reports thereafter, while the prescribed reward and
3358: queue operations remain defined.
3359: 
3360: \begin{algorithm}[p]
3361: \caption{Activation-Budgeted Maintenance (ABM)}
3362: \label{alg:abm}
3363: \begin{algorithmic}[1]
3364: \Require $\varepsilon,\delta,\rho_c,\rho_f\in(0,1)$; integer $s\ge0$
3365: \State Initialize empty coarse and fine FIFO queues, remaining budget $s$, fine rank $r=0$
3366: \For{physical time $t=1,2,\ldots$}
3367:  \State Receive the new batch; assign arrival-order labels and initialize lifetime counts and sums
3368:  \State Set each new arm's coarse quota $q_i^c$; append it to the coarse queue
3369:  \State Remove completed tasks from the fronts of both queues
3370:  \If{$t$ is even and the coarse queue is nonempty}
3371:    \State Select its front arm
3372:  \ElsIf{$t$ is even and the fine queue is nonempty}
3373:    \State Select its front arm
3374:  \Else
3375:    \State Select a largest full-history UCB index, using smallest-label ties
3376:  \EndIf
3377:  \State Observe one reward and update the selected arm's lifetime statistics
3378:  \State Update its fine block if active before this observation and not yet complete
3379:  \State At a quota crossing, form its corresponding coarse or fine interval
3380:  \If{this observation newly completes all coarse checkpoints in its batch}
3381:    \State Let $j$ be the batch's largest saved coarse mean, with smallest-label ties
3382:    \State Select other batch arms in label order, up to the remaining candidate budget
3383:    \If{at least one candidate was selected}
3384:      \State Activate $j$ first, then the selected candidates, each with the next rank $r$
3385:      \State Give rank $r$ quota $q_r^f$, zero fresh count and sum, and append its task
3386:      \State Decrease the remaining budget by the number of selected candidates
3387:    \EndIf
3388:  \EndIf
3389:  \State Report from saved-interval intersections; an empty intersection gives persistent all-unresolved reports
3390: \EndFor
3391: \end{algorithmic}
3392: \end{algorithm}
3393: 
3394: The fresh-block Hoeffding bound remains valid conditional on activation
3395: history. The next unrevealed suffix of the selected iid arm stream
3396: retains its iid law, even when its identity and activation time depend
3397: on past data. Apply the conditional bound first at bounded activation
3398: times and then take expectations; a task never activated has no failure
3399: event. Fine-rank errors total at most
3400: $\sum_{r\ge1}\delta/\{2r(r+1)\}=\delta/2$.
3401: Together with coarse coverage, this gives full-model all-time label
3402: reliability at least $1-\delta$. This argument requires fresh
3403: post-activation observations and does not validate reassigning a
3404: smaller error budget to a previously selected old prefix.

3406: \subsection{A sufficiently separated window contract}
3407: 
3408: On $\Theta_H$, choose $\rho_c=v/8$ and $\rho_f=d/4=\eta/2$.
3409: The coarse precision is a selected service parameter, not an estimate
3410: that automatically learns $v$. Define the analysis quantities
3411: \[
3412:  Q_c=\left\lceil128v^{-2}\log\frac{4K(K+1)}\delta\right\rceil,
3413:  \quad Q_f(r)=\left\lceil32d^{-2}
3414:                          \log\frac{4r(r+1)}\delta\right\rceil,
3415: \]
3416: \[
3417:  L_c=2(M+1)Q_c,\quad L=L_c+2(M+1)Q_f(2m),
3418: \]
3419: \[
3420:  B_{\rm rec}=L_c\left\{\frac{(J+1)(J+2)}2+JM\right\},
3421:  \qquad H\ge4(L+B_{\rm rec}).
3422: \]
3423: Here $B_{\rm rec}$ is a recovery-area constant; it is not a best mean,
3424: reward sum, or F-CCP budget. The displayed condition defines this
3425: performance family and is not future information given to ABM.
3426: 
3427: \begin{theorem}[ABM area and unconditional reward cost]
3428: \label{thm:abm-upper}
3429: For $0\le s\le m$, ABM on this family satisfies
3430: \[
3431:  P\{E_{\rm lab},\mathcal U_T(\eta)
3432:                    \le(m-s)H+sL+B_{\rm rec}\}\ge1-\delta.
3433: \]
3434: For $s\ge1$,
3435: \[
3436:  \sup_{\Theta_H}ER_T\le R_0(T)+4\varepsilon sQ_f(2s),
3437: \]
3438: where
3439: \[
3440:  R_0(T)=2\varepsilon KQ_c+
3441:  128\left(\frac m\varepsilon+\frac{J^2}{v}\right)\log(2T)+1.
3442: \]
3443: For $s=0$, the upper bound is $R_0(T)$ without a fine-layer term.
3444: \end{theorem}
3445: 
3446: \begin{proof}
3447: For every reward path, a batch requires at most $(M+1)Q_c$ coarse
3448: observations and $(M+1)Q_f(2m)$ fine fresh observations. Even-round
3449: service, helped by ordinary observations, completes them within $L$
3450: rounds of birth. Since $H\ge4(L+B_{\rm rec})$, induction over batches
3451: leaves no queue backlog at the next arrival. This workload statement
3452: does not rely on choosing the correct empirical reference.
3453: 
3454: On simultaneous coverage, each candidate is below its batch reference
3455: by at least $\varepsilon-d$, so the saved coarse means select the true
3456: reference. There are exactly $s$ selected candidates. Upon fine
3457: completion, the selected candidate and reference endpoints have errors
3458: at most $\rho_f$; $2\rho_f<d$ resolves their birth-window labels.
3459: No old coarse upper endpoint can obstruct this fine comparison:
3460: successive reference spacing satisfies $v>\rho_c+\rho_f$, and all
3461: candidates are farther below the current reference. Thus these $s$
3462: candidates contribute at most $sL$ in their birth windows, while the
3463: others contribute at most $(m-s)H$.
3464: 
3465: The new batch's coarse evidence completes within $L_c$ rounds.
3466: Because $d+2\rho_c<v$, its reference lower endpoint and the preceding
3467: batch's saved candidate upper endpoints exclude that older cohort
3468: without further old-candidate observations. Older exclusions persist.
3469: Every reference has gap at most $\varepsilon/4$ and combined coarse
3470: endpoint error at most $2\rho_c<\varepsilon/2$; all references are
3471: therefore positively certified after the current coarse layer
3472: completes. In window $e=0,\ldots,J$, this recovery can temporarily
3473: affect at most $e+1$ references and the preceding candidate batch.
3474: Its total area is bounded by
3475: \[
3476:  L_c\sum_{e=0}^J(e+1+M_{e-1})=B_{\rm rec},
3477:  \qquad M_{-1}=M_J=0.
3478: \]
3479: This proves the joint area bound.
3480: 
3481: For expected regret, do not condition on interval coverage. Every
3482: observation below its coarse quota is charged among at most $KQ_c$
3483: observations, each with gap at most $2\varepsilon$. All designated
3484: fine observations beyond these coarse counts are bounded by
3485: $2sQ_f(2s)$, and each again costs at most $2\varepsilon$.
3486: The remaining observations are ordinary full-history UCB pulls.
3487: Candidate arms have minimum positive gap at least $3\varepsilon/4$
3488: and maximum gap at most $2\varepsilon$; older references have minimum
3489: positive gap at least $v$ and maximum gap at most $Jv$.
3490: The distinct-count argument in Section~\ref{app:joint}, with its
3491: summable bad-round loss below 1, is bounded by
3492: $128(m/\varepsilon+J^2/v)\log(2T)+1$. First observations were already
3493: charged to the coarse quotas. These counts remain valid on failure
3494: paths, including paths on which a wrong empirical reference is chosen.
3495: This gives the unconditional reward bound.
3496: \end{proof}

3498: \subsection{A matching interval with an explicit additive cost}
3499: 
3500: \begin{theorem}[Intermediate area budgets on $\Theta_H$]
3501: \label{thm:abm-intermediate}
3502: Set $A_{\min}=mL+B_{\rm rec}$. For
3503: $A_{\min}\le a<V=mH$, let
3504: \[
3505:  s(a)=\left\lceil\frac{V+B_{\rm rec}-a}{H-L}\right\rceil.
3506: \]
3507: Then $1\le s(a)\le m$, ABM with candidate budget $s(a)$ attains area
3508: budget $a$, and
3509: \[
3510:  \frac{\varepsilon k(a)}{3072d^2}\log\frac{k(a)}\delta
3511:  \le\mathfrak R_{\Theta_H}(a)
3512:  \le R_0(T)+1024\frac{\varepsilon k(a)}{d^2}
3513:                                 \log\frac{k(a)}\delta.
3514: \]
3515: \end{theorem}
3516: \begin{proof}
3517: The premise $a\ge mL+B_{\rm rec}$ gives $s(a)\le m$, and its
3518: definition guarantees
3519: $(m-s)H+sL+B_{\rm rec}\le a$. If $k=k(a)$, then $V-a\le kH$.
3520: The separation condition gives
3521: \[
3522:  \frac{V+B_{\rm rec}-a}{H-L}
3523:        \le\frac{kH+B_{\rm rec}}{H-L}\le2k.
3524: \]
3525: Since $2k$ is an integer, $s(a)\le2k$. For $s\ge1$,
3526: \[
3527:  \log\frac{4(2s)(2s+1)}\delta\le3\log(s/\delta),
3528:  \quad Q_f(2s)\le97d^{-2}\log(s/\delta).
3529: \]
3530: Also $\log(2k/\delta)\le(5/4)\log(k/\delta)$. The fine term in
3531: Theorem~\ref{thm:abm-upper} is therefore bounded by
3532: \[
3533:  388\varepsilon s d^{-2}\log(s/\delta)
3534:        \le970\varepsilon k d^{-2}\log(k/\delta),
3535: \]
3536: which is at most the stated expression with constant 1024. The lower
3537: bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:abm-area-lower}.
3538: \end{proof}
3539: 
3540: For the explicit choice $H=16(L+B_{\rm rec})$,
3541: \[
3542:  \frac{A_{\min}}V\le\frac{1+1/m}{16}\le\frac3{32}<\frac18.
3543: \]
3544: Thus this separately calibrated family includes $V/8$ in its matching
3545: interval and extends through intermediate $a<V$. At fixed
3546: $J,M,\varepsilon,v,\delta$ and $d\downarrow0$, it has
3547: $H,T=\Theta(d^{-2})$ and $R_0(T)=O(\log(1/d))$; the fine term is at
3548: least of order $d^{-2}$. The additive $R_0(T)$ remains part of the
3549: finite-sample statement and need not be negligible in joint growth
3550: regimes.
3551: 
3552: The result does not characterize $a<A_{\min}$, the interval
3553: $[V,V+B_{\rm rec})$, arbitrary overlapping arrivals, or the full
3554: unknown-schedule frontier. For $a\ge V+B_{\rm rec}$, the coarse
3555: strategy supplies an upper bound without a matching lower bound here.
3556: ABM given $s$ uses no future arrivals; calibrating $s(a)$ from $a$
3557: uses the performance-family values $m,H$. It is therefore not an
3558: automatic future-free area-budget controller. Neither ABM's ordinary
3559: UCB guarantee nor this intermediate interval is asserted for F-CCP.
