S1.1    Exact scope and executable conventions
Fix a deterministic physical horizon T ≥ 1 and 0 < δ < 1. There are finitely many available arms
by every finite time, at least one arm is available at time one, and arrivals are deterministic and
permanent. The arm streams are independent, and each consists of iid [0, 1]-valued rewards with
mean ri . The proof also applies conditionally on an independent exogenous arrival schedule. No
gap-stability or cumulative-jump assumption is imposed. The learner observes the arrivals before
selecting the arm at each physical time. Newly arriving arms are buffered until the next algorithmic
round. All observations are retained, and an eliminated arm is not sampled again.

Audit and boundaries of the repair. The proof uses only bounded-stream concentration,
strict interval comparisons, the roundwise survivor-count invariant, a finite sum, and Cauchy–
Schwarz; each dependency is proved above. The competing route of terminal-gap compression is
unnecessary and is not reinstated. Ties, empty new batches, the first two rounds, singleton active
sets, deterministic rewards, arbitrary within-round benchmark jumps, and an unfinished first or last
target are all covered. The historical-deficit term vanishes only on the common confidence event.
The delay term is retained explicitly, including at the endpoint. The unknown-horizon wrapper
restarts the original screening algorithm and does not claim history retention across epochs. Neither
result establishes that the original smaller empirical calibration has the same error probability, that
the withdrawn gap-dependent corollary is true, or that the old round-based online theorem has been
repaired without changing its clock.
