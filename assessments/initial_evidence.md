# Frozen evidence for innovation assessment, initial batch

Scope: completed follow-up work as supplied on 15 October 2026, before the present round of extensions, manuscript writing, and comparative experiments. Evaluate innovation only. Do not infer a desired score from a target journal. No earlier assessments form part of this packet.

The companion `initial_theory.txt` contains the original research note through its literature discussion, excluding its numerical assessment. The mathematical statements and proofs, including limitations, are the primary evidence. The original manuscript considered stationary independent bounded rewards, exogenous permanent arm arrivals, one observation per physical round, screening, and dynamic regret against the currently available best arm. Its round-based regret claims have unresolved counterexamples and cannot be inherited by the checkpoint policy.

The follow-up keeps that observation model and adds a precise reporting target. At every post-observation time it partitions available arms into certified good, certified bad, and unresolved. Simultaneous familywise correctness is required over the entire path. The service loss sums unresolved arm-time only when the true distance to the relative epsilon boundary exceeds eta. Expected pseudo-regret and a high-probability bound on this area are distinct requirements. Policies do not observe means, the best-arm identity, future arrivals, or the terminal horizon.

Assess these fixed objects, retaining their interactions and avoiding double counting:

| ID | Contribution and role |
|---|---|
| c1 | Core: broadcast uncertainty lower bound T2, queue lemma, and polynomial arrival exponent C1/C2. A new arm's uncertain high mean simultaneously prevents positive certification of old candidates. All times use one common reference environment. Necessary K(t)=o(sqrt(t)); sufficient K(T)^2 log K(T)=o(T); exact exponent 1/2 for polynomial paths, with logarithmic boundary unresolved. |
| c2 | Core: temporary classification tasks T4/T6. The same area protocol forces completion in enough windows. A compatible product prior permits summing information costs. On an explicit repeated-update family with long, specified windows, fast service has matching expected regret of order epsilon J M d^{-2} log(M/delta). A larger constant-factor area budget admits coarse service with logarithmic dependence on 1/d when other parameters are fixed. This is a two-budget separation, not the full Pareto frontier. |
| c3 | Supporting method: CP-UCB-AA and T5. Lifetime fixed checkpoints, FIFO service interleaved with full-history UCB, shared observations, immediate admission, and reuse of old intervals. New benchmark evidence can retire an old cohort without resampling it. Unconditional regret does not condition on the reporting event. No exclusive advantage over history-retaining ALL-epsilon adaptations has been proved. |
| c4 | Supporting theory: T8/C5/T9. Worst-schedule maintenance area matches min(KT, K^2 eta^{-2} log(K/delta)); a first-round batch supplies its lower bound. A sparse but bursty infinite schedule shows K(t)=o(sqrt(t)) alone is insufficient for a uniform service guarantee. Distinguish fixed-path and horizonwise worst-path quantifiers. |
| overall | Overall current innovation, determined by the interacting core contributions rather than averaging c1-c4. |

Other results are supporting tools: standard confidence sequences and KL inequalities, information-allocation relaxation T1 without an attainability theorem, partial simultaneous recovery L3 with classical multiple-testing scales, and eventual finite-arm consistency T7. The submitted code implements constant precision only. Its diagnostics and one 20,000-round sanity run do not establish comparative performance or frequency coverage. No independent temporal-geometry/minimax-regret paper is included.

Nearest primary sources checked for this batch:

1. Mason, Jain, Tripathy and Nowak (2020), *Finding All epsilon-Good Arms in Stochastic Bandits*, NeurIPS. The fixed-arm target is exactly the relative-good set; Theorem 2.1 already separates boundary discrimination from uncertainty about the best value. ST2 combines threshold learning and boundary sampling; FAREAST is instance optimal. This work already explains that one uncertain arm can prevent certification elsewhere in a static problem. It does not state a continual arrival-area theorem. [Paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/edf0320adc8658b25ca26be5351b6c4a-Paper.pdf)
2. Ghalme et al. (2021), *Ballooning multi-armed bandits*, Artificial Intelligence 296, 103485. Permanent expanding sets and regret against the current best are established. Their feasibility and algorithms involve restrictions on best-arm arrival distributions; an unrestricted arrival model can have linear regret. No claim that this model or comparator is new is admissible. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10055)
3. Cho, Meier, Gan and Kallus (2025), *Reward Maximization for Pure Exploration*, AISTATS 258, 2251-2259. Reward-maximizing sampling combined with anytime-valid testing already exists for fixed-threshold good-arm identification. The reporting/allocation modularity alone is not a new principle. [Official publication](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/cho25a.html)
4. Yang, Tan and Jin (2026), *Best Arm Identification with Minimal Regret*, JMLR 27(86). Fixed-confidence single-best-arm identification studies sampling and regret tradeoffs. It is a direct comparator for joint objectives, rather than evidence that such a joint objective is new. [Primary full text](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/24-1612/24-1612.pdf)
5. Degenne et al. (2019), *Bridging the gap between regret minimization and best arm identification*, AISTATS 89, 1988-1996. Joint cost, correctness and decision-time tradeoffs already exist. [Official publication](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/degenne19a.html)
6. Malloy and Nowak (2014), *Sequential Testing for Sparse Recovery*, IEEE TIT 60, 7862-7873; Jamieson and Jain (2018), sequential experimental design with false discovery control; Cheshire, Menard and Carpentier (2020), thresholding bandits. Sequential simultaneous-recovery logarithms are prior statistical structure. Malloy and Nowak's coordinatewise assumptions do not directly prove arbitrary interleaving and partial-stopping claims. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1801), [official thresholding paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/cheshire20a.html).

Verification limits: targeted primary-source search, not an exhaustive novelty proof; the mathematical note has written proofs and diagnostics, with independent audit in progress. Finite-window separation constants are conservative and implied horizons can be very large.
